Case study · Telematics · Apr 2022 – Apr 2024

Supporting a Wialon telematics service for 1,700 vehicles

At Track Monitoring Service, a regional Wialon partner in Uzbekistan, I was part of the small team that kept the platform working: trackers, fuel sensors, unit setup, client accounts and the paperwork behind every installation.

Scope

~1,700vehicles and machines on the Wialon Hosting platform I supported
250+client accounts across two Wialon servers
~160fuel tank calibrations by our team, many of which I worked on
139tracker configuration templates in our shared library

Clients were construction companies, road maintenance units, utilities, municipal services, farms and private hauliers. The fleet was mostly heavy equipment: dump trucks, excavators, wheel loaders, cranes, concrete mixers, graders, tractors and combine harvesters. For most of these clients fuel was the reason to buy tracking, so fuel accuracy was the job.

Hardware was Teltonika: about two thirds FMB920, then FMB125 for vehicles with fuel sensors, plus an older FM1120 / FM1125 / FM5300 base still in service.

What I did

Tracker provisioning at scale

Teltonika · Configuration

  • Worked with and extended a shared library of 139 ready configs, one per combination of tracker model, mobile operator (four APNs), number of fuel sensors (0–4), RS-232 or RS-485 and roaming.
  • A new install meant picking the right template instead of configuring from scratch, which cut setup time and mistakes.
  • Firmware updates across the FMB1xx / FMB920 range.
  • FMB920
  • FMB125
  • FM11xx / FM5300
  • Teltonika Configurator

Fuel level monitoring

LLS sensors · Calibration

  • Capacitive fuel level sensors (Escort TD-150, Omnicomm) on RS-485, one to four per vehicle depending on the number and shape of tanks.
  • Calibrated each tank by filling it in measured steps and recording the raw sensor value, then loaded the table into the Wialon fuel sensor.
  • Tuned fill and theft detection (minimum volume, join interval, stop timeout) so reports showed real drains, not sloshing.
  • Escort TD-150
  • Omnicomm LLS
  • RS-485
  • Fuel reports

Wialon unit and account setup

Wialon Hosting · Administration

  • Unit profile templates (.wlp) with sensors, trip detection, fuel consumption rules and report settings, reused across similar machines.
  • Client accounts, users, rights, unit groups and custom fields recording install date, tracker serial, seals and installer for every unit.
  • Sensor presets for BLE accessories: temperature, door, angle and Technoton flow meters.

Example: calibrating a two-sensor tank

Real data from one of our calibrations: a wheeled excavator with a 440 L tank and two sensors, filled in 20 L steps. Two sensors cover the tank tilt when the machine stands on a slope; Wialon averages them.

Sensor 1Sensor 2

22 points per sensor. The curves are almost straight because the tank is a rectangular box; on cylindrical or shaped tanks the same method gives a clearly curved table, which is why each tank was calibrated rather than calculated.

Why it matters for security work

A telematics platform is an IoT system with a lot of attack surface: hundreds of SIM-connected devices talking to a public server, client logins, and data that companies treat as sensitive (where their machines are and how much fuel they burn). Running it taught me to keep an inventory of every device, control who can see which units, notice devices that go quiet, and document every change.

That is the same discipline I now apply in server audits and in my security tools.
